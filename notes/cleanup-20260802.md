# Post-review cleanup — 2026-08-02

Remediation of the data damage found by `notes/codereview.md`. The owner paused
auditioning for this ("it takes me longer to physically listen to the files, so
it's better to be sure of the value"), so the ordering here is: repair the
corpus first, then spend ears.

The headline: **almost none of the damage required discarding clips.** Two
clips were dropped in total. Everything else was either a metadata correction
or a deterministic re-cut from sources still on disk.

## 1. Phoneme corpus — rebuilt, not pruned (D-C1)

`data/libritts_r_vat_v3b/` replaces v3 as the corpus to train on. Built with
`scripts/rephonemize_corpus.py`, which regenerates only the phoneme column and
carries over the speaker index, the VAT labels and the exact train/val
membership (verified identical).

| corpus | rows | rows with poisoned IPA |
|---|---|---|
| libritts_r_vat (v1) | 30,351 | 1,956 (6.4%) |
| libritts_r_vat_v2 | 30,351 | 1,956 (6.4%) |
| libritts_r_vat_v3 | 31,445 | 1,993 (6.3%) |
| **libritts_r_vat_v3b** | **31,445** | **2 (source-text defects)** |

Full diff v3 → v3b: 2,981 rows (9.5%) and 3,560 tokens corrected. The two
residual rows are LibriTTS-R **transcript** defects — the source literally
writes "Im going to look this thing up" and "them thats done it" without
apostrophes — not G2P failures.

Worst corrections by frequency: `don't` dˈɔnt→dˈoʊnt (518), `it's` ˈɪts→ɪts
(249), `didn't` dˈɪdnt→dˈɪdənt (197), `I'm` **ˈɪm→ˈaɪm** (191, was rhyming with
*him*), `I'll` **ˈɪl→ˈaɪl** (125, was *ill*), `that's` **θˈæts→ðæts** (122,
voiceless th), `won't` wˈɔnt→wˈoʊnt (107), `I'd` **ˈɪˈː→ˈaɪd** (51, was not a
word at all).

`data/LJSpeech-1.1` carries the same defect at ~1.2% of rows and has NOT been
rebuilt — it feeds the Phase-0 warm-start checkpoint, not the VAT lane.

**Nothing was trained on v3.** `configs/experiment/vat3_finetune.yaml` overrides
to `libritts_r_vat_v2.yaml` and no v3 config was ever written, so the poison was
caught before it reached a checkpoint. The existing
`checkpoint_epoch=099.ckpt` did train on v2 and therefore on ~6.4% poisoned rows.

### Train/val split leakage — RESOLVED 2026-08-02, corpus is now v3c

v2-val and v3-val share only **19 of ~910 clips**; 889 of v3's val clips sit in
v2's train set. Cross-version val-loss comparisons were invalid and evaluating
the v2 checkpoint against v3/v3b val leaked. Sorting rows before the shuffle
fixed the *assembly-order* half of it; what it could not fix is growth, because a
shuffle assigns membership by POSITION — add one clip and every clip after it
re-rolls.

**Owner chose the full fix: per-clip hash assignment.** `derive_vat_corpus._in_val`
now decides membership from a blake2b hash of the wav BASENAME (globally unique in
LibriTTS-R; keying on the absolute path would re-roll the split the day the corpus
moves directories). `SEED` is deleted along with the shuffle it seeded —
`SPLIT_SALT = "sonora-vat-split-20260802"` is the only thing that re-rolls a split
now, and it carries its date because changing it costs every prior checkpoint's
comparability.

`scripts/resplit_corpus.py` applies it to a corpus already built, moving rows
between the two filelists and touching nothing else. It refuses to write over its
source: the old split is the record of what the existing checkpoint trained on.

| | v3b (shipped) | **v3c** |
|---|---|---|
| rows | 31,445 | 31,445 (identical) |
| val | 943 (2.998%) | **960 (3.05%)** |
| membership rule | position in a seeded shuffle | hash of the clip |
| val speakers | — | 210 of 247, max 16 clips from one |

Verified: adding 2,000 synthetic rows moved **0 of 960** val clips. Under the old
rule that same growth re-rolled essentially the whole set.

The trade is that val size is binomial rather than exact — ±30 clips at this
size. That is the right thing to give up: an exactly-sized val set that means
something different every version is worth less than a slightly ragged one that
does not move.

**v3c is the corpus to train on.** v3b stays on disk unchanged. No training config
points at v3c yet — and `data_statistics` (mel mean/std) in the v2 config were
computed on the 29,441-clip split, so the v3 lineage's 31,445 rows need
`matcha/utils/generate_data_statistics.py` re-run **inside the training container**
before a run. Do not inherit v2's numbers silently.

## 2. LibriVox lane — re-cut, not pruned (A-C1)

The pad-before-clamp fix changes a clip's window only where a neighbouring
sentence fell within `PAD_SECONDS`; everywhere else the pad was capturing
silence and the window is byte-identical. So the lane was re-aligned from the
source mp3s rather than discarded.

**librivox-speech-v1** (Dickens, *Speeches: Literary and Social*, Bryn Roberts):

- 79 clips before, 79 after — the re-cut is deterministic, same clip set
- **69 of 79 windows unchanged**; 10 moved, all shorter; 2.92 s of audio removed
- Worst case `lv003_0065` started **1.782 s early**, carrying most of the
  previous sentence
- QC: 78/79 pass before and after, **zero regressions**
- Independent confirmation the fix is right: mean ASR WER on the moved clips
  fell **0.043 → 0.026**, and `lv003_0065` went 0.167 → 0.067. The removed audio
  was speech the transcript did not contain, which is exactly what WER sees.
- **Zero heard verdicts invalidated.** 7 audited clips moved; all 7 were
  machine-folds that no human had listened to (checked against
  `ratings_history.csv`).

Old cut archived at `librivox-speech-v1.precut-20260802`; `staging_log.json`
carried across so the lane cannot be double-staged.

swapped. It also picks up the duration-split slicing fix (`f4897b5`), which
changed which text lands in which section — and clip ids are

|---|---|---|
| clips | 664 | 1,366 |
| audio | 1.50 h | 3.13 h |
| sections | 15 of 25 | 25 of 25 |

**111 ids exist in both pools and every single one points at different audio**
(t0 differs by more than 0.5 s in 111 of 111). 11 of the 12 audited
`book-librivox-v1` clips are not reproduced at all; the twelfth keeps its id

Swapping would therefore have silently repointed audited rows at audio nobody

- `librivox-v1` **stays as it is**, edge defect and all, and its 12 ear

- `librivox-v2` is the better pool going forward: correct clip boundaries,

  librivox-v2`), which would write rows as `book-librivox-v2`.

Retire `librivox-v1` whenever re-earning those 12 verdicts is worth it; there
is no rush and no contamination risk in leaving both.

## 4. teacher-ab-v1 normalized (loudness confound)

The campaign that set the teacher hierarchy was auditioned at **raw engine
level**. Across its 56 keeps the per-engine median spread was **5.99 dB** (8.57
dB across all 80 including drops) — and Qwen sat **3.81 dB louder than
VibeVoice**, which is precisely the comparison that made Qwen the gold standard.

All 56 keeps normalized to −23.0 LUFS; spread now **0.00 dB**. Originals in
`audio/_pre_loudnorm/`.

This does not by itself overturn anything — Dia was the *loudest* engine
(−18.6) and still scored worst, so the loudness ordering does not track the
verdicts. It makes a re-test possible, which it previously was not.

**Caveat:** only the 56 keeps are normalized. A keep-*rate* re-test would need
the 24 quarantined drops normalized too, which means un-quarantining them —
left as an owner call.

## The QC gate had a blind spot, and it is now closed

Both clips the ear dropped **passed every gate** — WER 0.00 and 0.045, DNSMOS
3.3, duration fine. The defect was internal dead air, which no instrument in
the gate could see. `silero_vad.py` was written for exactly this ("3.5 second
pause between 'patience.' and 'We asked for time.' ... the energy gate never
flagged either") and had been wired into nothing since 2026-07-29.

Calibrated against 369 already-audited clips across three campaigns — 303
keeps and 21 drops whose ear note names a pause:

| threshold | catches | flags of keeps |
|---|---|---|
| 1.40 s | 20 of 21 (95%) | 3.3% |
| 2.50 s | 15 of 21 (71%) | 0.0% |

Shipped as two levels: a hard gate at **2.5 s**, where no good clip has ever
landed and the observed defects run 3–8 s with a rushed resumption after; and
an advisory band from **1.4 s**, which queues the clip for the ear rather than
condemning it, because a long pause is sometimes a real dramatic choice. The
one drop 1.4 s misses has no measurable pause at all, so 95% is this
instrument's ceiling. A length-relative threshold was tested and matched — not
beat — the absolute one, so absolute won on legibility.

Gate agreement on newscaster-v1 before the fix: 74 agree-keep, 0 agree-drop,
**2 false negatives**, 2 false positives. Both false negatives are caught now.
